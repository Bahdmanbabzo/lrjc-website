Medical students are taught to be confident. We are taught the mechanisms of
disease as though they were settled, the indications for treatment as though the
evidence beneath them were permanent, and the guidelines as though they would
not be revised within a decade of our graduation.

Almost none of that is true, and almost none of it is our fault. What we are
rarely taught is how to hold a claim at the right strength — to say what a study
establishes, what it merely suggests, and what it cannot address at all.

This volume is a record of a year spent trying to do exactly that. Its contents
include one original audit, one methodological commentary, one student survey,
and a substantial number of pieces about how we are taught to read evidence and
how easily we misread it.

We have not tried to make these pages definitive. We have tried to make them
honest about their own limitations, which is the standard we ask of the papers
we appraise.

## A note on this volume

Volume I was assembled by the editorial board from work completed during the
2026 calendar year. Articles are published in the order in which they were
accepted, and each carries the name of every member who contributed.

Where an article's conclusions are narrower than the questions its authors
began with, we have preferred to say so.
